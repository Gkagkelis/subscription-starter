# Phase 5 literature: cross-national transfer of survey responses

Compiled 29 Oct 2026 for the Phase 5 data sprint. Each entry gives the citation, the one-line takeaway, and what it implies for the preregistered models (B = nearest countries, C = global country × column factorisation, C-S2 = new related questions). The takeaways come from abstracts and publisher pages that were checked this session. Full texts were not re-read, so effect sizes are as reported in the abstracts.

| 1 | Caughey, D., O'Grady, T. & Warshaw, C. (2019). *Policy Ideology in European Mass Publics, 1981–2016.* American Political Science Review 113(3), 674–693. doi:10.1017/S0003055419000157 | A dynamic group-level ordinal IRT model pools about 2.7 million responses to 109 different items across 27 European countries. ## Synthesis for the Phase 5 design

1. **Latent-factor pooling works for levels; new items are hard.** Papers 1–3 show that country embeddings from many partially overlapping items recover country positions well (errors of a few pp). Papers 6–7 show that accuracy drops sharply for questions never asked anywhere (S2).
2. **Composition before culture.** Papers 3–4 support post-stratifying foreign data to the Greek age × sex × education structure (§5) before estimating a country effect.
3. **Neighbour selection should be principled.** Paper 5 provides a WVS-based cultural-distance metric. In practice, the same metric can be computed on the shared EB, ESS, LiTS, QoG and CSES items in this lake.
4. **Respondent-level pretraining is complementary.** Papers 8–9 support training on many (subgroup × item) distributions across survey families. This is what the expanded lake (EB trend file, ESS R3/R6–R9, PIAAC, LiTS I–III, CSES IMD, QoG EQI 2013/2017) makes possible.
