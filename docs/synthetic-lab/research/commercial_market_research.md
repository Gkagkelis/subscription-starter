## Q1. Greek market-research firms and industry bodies: what each publishes openly vs. paid

### Takeaway
Greek commercial research reaches the public almost only as **press releases and summary PDFs** (headline percentages, sometimes split by age, gender or region). Respondent-level raw data is not released by any Greek commercial firm I checked. The richest free consumer releases come from **industry bodies and associations** (IELKA, GR.EC.A./ELTRUN, ESEE/IME, IOBE), not from the agencies' own sites. The agencies (MRB, Focus Bari, Marc, Metron, Pulse, Prorata, Interview, GPO, Alco, Opinion Poll) mostly publish political or social barometers commissioned by media, and their consumer panels are sold to clients.

## Q2. International sources: free vs paid and their reuse terms

### Takeaway
DataReportal is free to read but **not** freely reusable in a commercial product: paid or commercial reuse needs permission and a fee, and no raw data is provided. Statista grants no publication rights for paid content without a Professional or Enterprise licence. Google Trends allows reuse with attribution "subject to the Google Terms of Service". The official API is alpha and access is gated, and the unofficial pytrends library was archived in Apr 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **DataReportal "Digital 2026: Greece"** (published 8 Nov 2025, Kepios/Simon Kemp, with We Are Social and Meltwater) — [datareportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-greece)
  - Population 9.93m, median age 46.8, 81.4% urban.
  - Internet users 8.64m (87.0%); 1.29m offline.
  - Social media user identities 7.40m (74.5%; not unique persons).
  - Cellular connections 13.7m (138%; GSMA Intelligence).
  - Median mobile download 120.53 Mbps; fixed 71.76 Mbps (Ookla, Aug 2025).
  - Ad reach: YouTube 7.40m (74.5%); Facebook 5.10m (51.4%); Instagram 4.60m (46.3%); TikTok 4.60m adults 18+ (55.3% of adults).
  - The report advises against computing change over time from earlier reports.
- DataReportal terms: "all of the content on DataReportal.com is protected by copyright"; footer reads "© Kepios. All rights reserved." — [legal](https://datareportal.com/legal)
- DataReportal reuse rules — [citation & reuse](https://datareportal.com/citation)
  - Free republication of individual pages is allowed under "fair dealing" (Singapore law) only if no access fee is charged and pages are reproduced in full in their original design with attribution and a link.
  - "[W]e charge a fee to process requests to reuse our materials in publications", and requests need a "minimum of four weeks". Paid publications, products and services need permission.
  - Recreating 10 or more charts or pages requires contacting them first.
  - DataReportal is "unable to provide any of the raw data". Contact: reports@kepios.com, subject "Reusing Content".
- **Statista** — [Statista publication rights](https://www.statista.com/getting-started/publishing-statista-content-terms-of-use-and-publication-rights)
  - Registered users get "simple usage rights to the downloaded statistics for private use".
  - Commercial use is limited to Professional, Enterprise and Campus licences.
  - "No publication rights whatsoever are granted with regard to content that is only available against payment."
  - Statista Greece pages mostly re-host third-party data (Eurostat, ELSTAT, etc.), so the original source should be cited instead.
- **Google Trends** — [Google Trends help](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365538?hl=en)
  - "You can use any information from Google Trends, subject to the Google Terms of Service. If you reuse Trends data, attribute the information to Google with a citation", e.g. "Data source: Google Trends (https://www.google.com/trends)".
  - Do not cite Trends together with Google Ads data.
- Google Trends API: announced 24 Jul 2025 as alpha with application-based access. — [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/07/trends-api?hl=it)
  - The unofficial pytrends repository was archived on 17 Apr 2025. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/esteban_ortega/pytrends-is-dead-heres-how-to-get-google-trends-data-in-2026-1a18)
  - Scraping the Trends UI may breach Google ToS on automated access. This is general ToS knowledge and was not quoted from the ToS this session **[UNVERIFIED quote]**.
- **Euromonitor, GWI, GSMA Intelligence, Kantar Media**: paid subscription products. Their headline figures reach the public only through partner reports such as DataReportal (which cites GSMA Intelligence). Their terms were not fetched **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Meta/Google ad-audience tools**: DataReportal itself uses "planning tools from Google and Meta" for reach figures. These are advertiser-account tools whose ToS generally restrict extraction. **[UNVERIFIED terms]** — [datareportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-greece)
- **Ookla/Speedtest**: Greek median speeds are public via DataReportal. Ookla's open-data tiles and licence were not checked **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### Inferences
- A product can cite DataReportal or Statista numbers (single facts with attribution) as validation targets. It cannot republish their charts, nor bulk-recreate 10 or more charts, without a licence.
- Google Trends is the only international behavioural signal that is programmatically usable on free terms, but only via the official alpha API or with care regarding ToS. Normalised indices (0–100) are fine as relative priors.

### Gaps
- Full Google ToS text on automated queries, GWI and Euromonitor licence terms, and the Ookla Open Data licence were not fetched.

## Q3. Legal frame: copyright, the EU sui generis database right, TDM, and what a product may do

### Takeaway
Individual facts and statistics (e.g. "40% cite food as the biggest burden") are not protected by copyright. Report texts, charts, layouts and infographics are. Systematic extraction of a **substantial part** of a database, or repeated extraction of insubstantial parts, can infringe the sui generis right (Dir. 96/9/EC art. 7; Greek L.2121/1993 art. 45A), where the maker made a substantial investment. Commercial TDM is allowed under Art. 4 of Dir. 2019/790 (transposed in Greece by L.4996/2022) **unless the rightholder has opted out** in machine-readable form or in its terms.

### Cited Findings
- Directive 96/9/EC art. 7 lets the database maker prevent extraction and/or re-utilisation of all or a substantial part of the contents, where there was substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting the contents. Repeated and systematic extraction of insubstantial parts that conflicts with normal exploitation is also barred. — [EUR-Lex 96/9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1996/9/2019-06-06/eng); [legislation.gov.uk ch. III](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1996/9/chapter/III)
- Directive 2019/790 Recital 9: TDM can implicate copyright and the sui generis right, and authorisation is needed absent an exception. TDM on facts or data not protected by copyright needs no copyright authorisation. Arts 3 (research) and 4 (any purpose, subject to opt-out) are the TDM exceptions. — [legislation.gov.uk 2019/790](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/790/introduction/data.html); commentary on CJEU *CV-Online Latvia v Melons* (C-762/19) — [ULL](https://ull.es/revistas/index.php/derecho/article/download/6384/4315)
- Greek law: L.2121/1993 art. 45A implements the sui generis right. L.4996/2022 added TDM limitations to art. 45A(6), transposing arts 3–6 of Dir. 2019/790. The article numbering of the amending provision differs between sources, so check the ΦΕΚ. — [taxheaven L.4996/2022](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4996/2022/arthro/30); [lawspot L.2121/1993](https://www.lawspot.gr/nomothesia/nomos-2121-1993/); [Your Europe (EL)](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/running-business/intellectual-property/database-protection/index_el.htm)
- Athens Multi-Member Court of First Instance 4166/2019 considered whether exercising the art. 45A §3 right could amount to abuse of dominance. The holding was not confirmed from the snippet. — [OPI library](https://library.opi.gr/wp-content/uploads/4166_2019.pdf)

### Inferences (not legal advice)
- **Allowed with low risk:** citing individual published figures with attribution ("Source: IELKA, March 2026, n=800") as calibration or validation targets; storing a small curated table of such toplines, with source URLs, as reference metadata.
- **Needs a licence or caution:**
  - Bulk-ingesting or reproducing full reports or PDFs, charts or infographics (copyright).
  - Systematically scraping a publisher's archive of releases into a database (sui generis risk, plus Art. 4 opt-outs in site ToS).
  - Republishing DataReportal or Statista charts inside a paid product.
  - Using paid panel data (NIQ, Kantar, GWI, Euromonitor) beyond the licence.
- **Preferred open alternatives** with explicit reuse licences: EU Commission BCS consumer survey data (IOBE is the Greek partner). Commission content is generally reusable with attribution under Decision 2011/833/EU; this was **not re-verified this session**, so confirm on the [Commission legal notice](https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en).

### Gaps
- Full text of art. 45A and of the L.4996/2022 provision not retrieved.
- Whether Greek publishers (IELKA, GR.EC.A., MRB) have declared TDM opt-outs (robots.txt or ToS) was not checked.

### Inferences
- These toplines give marginal targets (often split by age, sometimes by region) for calibrating a synthetic population. Joint distributions are not available from any of them.
- Sample frames differ (online buyers vs nationwide phone vs retail stores), so each should be kept as a separate validation target.
